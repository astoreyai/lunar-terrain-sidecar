# ADR 0006 — Far-field horizon ring from LDEM_75S, opt-in, merged by per-bin max

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-08-04

## Context

`terrain.getHorizon` ray-marches the widest configured layer, so skyline
fidelity was bounded by that layer's extent — a disclosed limitation
([known-limitations.md](../known-limitations.md)). At grazing polar sun the
bound is material: a 260 m massif at 10 km subtends the same 1.5° as a 13 m
ridge at 500 m, and real south-polar skylines are set by relief tens of
kilometres away (Mazarico, Neumann, Smith, Zuber & Torrence 2011, *Icarus*
211 — the reference method, which computes polar illumination from LOLA
gridded topography at these ranges). The error is one-directional: terrain
beyond the layer can only *raise* the horizon, so every consumer of the
near-field skyline errs bright.

The wide product needed is already a project data dependency: LOLA
`LDEM_75S` (120 m/px, 75°S–90°S, PDS3), read by the same `@lts/lunar-dem`
machinery that ingests it for layers.

## Decision

`packages/lunar-dem/src/farHorizon.ts` computes a 360-bin far-field skyline
by marching **true great circles** from the observer over the raw LDEM
radial elevations, with the **exact spherical elevation angle**

    el = atan2(rₚ·cos γ − r₀,  rₚ·sin γ),   γ = s / R

(no tangent-plane or d²/2R approximation; the observer's radial elevation is
reconstructed exactly from the dataset's measured datum plus the stored
tangent-plane height plus the curvature term ingestion removed).

`terrain.getHorizon` accepts an opt-in `farField` parameter (boolean or
`{ demPath?, maxRangeMeters? }`, default range 100 km). The response's
`horizonElevationDeg` becomes the **per-bin max** of near field and ring —
the fine layers resolve nearby rims the 120 m grid smooths away; the ring
contributes what the layers cannot see — with both inputs and a provenance
block (`source`, `observer`, ranges, truncation, method) returned alongside.

### Off by default

The near-field-only response is the documented v1.0 shape and its numbers
feed nothing that affects generated bytes; keeping the default unchanged
preserves both client compatibility and the reproduce contract. Consumers
that want physical skylines at grazing sun pass `farField: true`.

### Structured refusals, never fallbacks

- Missing product → `TERRAIN_DEM_UNAVAILABLE` naming the path tried and the
  `LTS_LDEM_75S` override. No near-field-only silent answer: a response that
  *claims* far-field coverage must have computed it.
- Procedural (non-DEM) dataset → `TERRAIN_INVALID_CONFIG`: the observer's
  radial elevation only exists when the datum is measured; a ring computed
  against a nominal datum would be real-data provenance wrapped around a
  physically meaningless number.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Always-on merge.** Changes every existing getHorizon response and makes
  the LDEM a hard runtime dependency of a query that previously needed none.
- **Resample-then-march in the local tangent frame** (reuse
  `resampleDemToLocal` + `horizonProfile`). At 100 km the local frame's
  straight rays and constant projection scale drift from great circles, and
  a ±100 km resample allocates ~2.8 M samples per query to answer 360 bins.
  The great-circle march reads one window and is exact at any range.
- **Precomputed horizon maps** (Mazarico et al.'s production approach).
  Right for mapping illumination over whole polar regions; this sidecar
  answers per-site queries in ~200 ms, so precomputation buys nothing yet.

## Verification (`tests/far-horizon.test.ts`, port 8816)

- Flat-sphere raster: every bin equals the closed form −γ/2 to 1e-9°; an
  elevated observer reproduces the classical horizon dip −acos(R/(R+h₀)).
- A wall placed due east by an independently hand-rolled great-circle step
  appears in bin 90 and not in bins 0/180/270 — the azimuth-mirror check a
  radially symmetric fixture cannot perform.
- Real LDEM_75S at Site01: bit-deterministic, 100 km untruncated, values
  pinned (max 0.70193° at azimuth 17°); 500 km range reports truncation at
  the 75°S product edge; an off-product observer throws.
- Protocol: back-compat shape without `farField`; merged = per-bin max with
  provenance and a measured-datum observer (~1.95 km radial at Site01);
  structured errors for missing product and procedural datasets.
- Cross-path check (recorded in the ADR only): per-sample elevation angles
  from the great-circle path agree with the tangent-plane ingestion path to
  ≤ 0.01° at 14 km once start ranges are matched.

## Bounds that remain

The ring is bounded by its source: 120 m/px smooths rims sharper than the
grid, so the merged skyline is still a *lower* bound near ridgelines, and
coverage ends at 75°S (reported via `truncatedAtM`, relevant only for
ranges ≳ 400 km from polar sites). The renderer's shadow map still uses
layer geometry only — the ring corrects horizon/illumination *queries*, not
the lit viewport; wiring `isSunlit`/`sunlitFraction` consumers and the UI to
it is listed as next work.
